Cities across India are experimenting with electric buses to cut air pollution and fuel costs. Transport
agencies say an electric bus costs more to buy than a diesel one, but is far cheaper to run because
electricity costs less per kilometre and there are fewer moving parts to maintain. Several cities now lease
buses from private operators and pay per kilometre, which lowers the upfront burden on public budgets.
Charging remains the main challenge. Depots need strong grid connections, and many older depots were not
designed with space for chargers. Planners are working with power utilities to upgrade connections and to
schedule charging at night, when electricity demand is low. Early results from pilot routes show lower noise
and fewer breakdowns, though operators note that battery range drops in extreme heat and when air
conditioning is running continuously. Officials say the next step is to expand charging at terminals so
that buses can top up between trips instead of returning to depots.
